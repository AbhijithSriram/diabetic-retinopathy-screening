Real-World Deployment Scenarios

Engineered for India's Primary Healthcare Realities

Theoretical algorithms fail when there is no internet or specialist nearby. DrishtiTrust is architected for the actual constraints of rural primary care.

USE CASE 01 · REMOTE CAMPS

Screening at the Primary Health Centre

A health worker photographs each eye; a server at the PHC or district grades it in seconds (PyTorch or MATLAB), after a quality check that asks for a retake when the photo is unreadable.

Impact: A result and a printed slip (English, Hindi, Tamil) before the patient leaves. Needs a network link to the grading server.
USE CASE 02 · TELE-TRIAGE

30-Second Specialist Capacity Multiplier

A district ophthalmologist cannot read every photograph. DrishtiTrust clears confident negatives, refers confident positives, and sends only the uncertain cases (plus a 2% random audit) to the specialist, with lesion overlays.

Impact: Simulated district of 100,000 screenings a year: about 23% need a specialist look, so one reviewer at 2 h a day clears every case within 48 h. Review time per case is assumed (30 s); the reader study that measures it has not been run yet.
USE CASE 03 · ESCORT CARE

Closing the Referral Loop

In the SMART India study, only 10% of 324 people found with sight-threatening DR attended treatment, despite repeated reminders (Kumar et al., PLoS One 2023). DrishtiTrust tracks every referral to attendance and escalates a missed visit from patient to family to the ASHA worker.

Impact: Referral tracker with a status link for the patient. SMS is mocked in this prototype (no gateway connected); attendance has not been measured.
System Architecture

From Rural Camp to Closed-Loop Hospital Care

A tele-screening pipeline for primary health centres: a quality gate at capture, AI grading on a server, specialist review for uncertain cases, and referral tracking.

01

Camp Capture

ASHA/ANM captures a fundus photograph; framing, exposure and focus rules plus a learned gradability score ask for a retake, up to three attempts.

3-Attempt Gate
02

Three-CNN Ensemble

EfficientNetV2-S at 512 and 768 px plus EfficientNet-B0 at 768 px, averaged; a U-Net segments lesions and a second net finds the disc and fovea. 1–4 s a photo on a GPU server.

Server-side
03

30s Tele-Review

Cases the AI is unsure about, and exudates near the fovea, go to the ophthalmologist queue with lesion overlays, Grad-CAM and the grade probabilities.

FIFO Auto-Queue
04

Escalated Care

Printed slip (English, Hindi, Tamil) with a status link; referral tracker escalates missed visits from patient to family to ASHA worker (SMS mocked).

Clinical Protocols & Standards

Frequently Answered Questions

Clinical guidelines, tele-ophthalmology safety rules, and patient privacy standards.

  • What is the clinical criteria for an immediate tele-review escalation?
    A photo is referred directly when the ensemble’s probability of referable DR is above a high threshold, and cleared for routine recall when it is below a low one; both thresholds are fitted by conformal risk control on held-out data so that at most 3% of referable patients are auto-cleared. Everything in between, and any photo with hard exudates within one disc diameter of the fovea, goes to the specialist queue. A case is marked urgent when its 90% prediction set reaches severe NPDR or worse, or when macular oedema is suspected. A random 2% of automatic recalls are also sent for review as a safety audically escalated.
  • Does DrishtiTrust work offline in camps with no 4G?
    It does not yet. Grading runs on a server (PyTorch or MATLAB), so a camp needs a network link to it; cases and referrals are stored in the server’s database. Offline capture with later upload is modelled in the district simulation but not built.
  • How is patient data protected under the Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act?
    This prototype stores no names: cases carry a patient code, and the patient’s status page sits behind an unguessable link. Photos are re-encoded after cropping, so camera metadata is not kept. Access control, encryption at rest and a DPDP Act compliance review are still to do before any real deployment.
